## 2. The problem

**H2**

> Why video is the thing that keeps slipping

- **Editing is a full-time skill you need part-time.** Hiring for it means a salary, equipment, software, management and cover when they are away — for output that varies month to month.
- **The content calendar bends around the edit.** Filming gets done. Publishing waits, then slides, then stops being a calendar.
- **Quality swings depending on who picked up the job.** Freelancer to freelancer, the pacing, the captions and the colour are all slightly different — and a brand that looks different every week reads as a brand that is not paying attention.
- **Nobody owns the feedback loop.** Revisions turn into an email chain between three people, and the video that was two days from finished is now three weeks old.

## 7. The strongest argument on this page is the one it cannot make

The pitch to a business owner is a **cost** pitch: an editing team costs less than an editor on staff. The source copy says this three times — *"without the hiring, management, or salary overhead"*, *"expensive in-house video editor salaries"*, *"on budget"* — and **never once puts a number to it.**

Under **R04** the site publishes per-unit rates ($15/min long-form, $25/reel, $15/min motion graphics — *not yet confirmed correct by Masud, per RULINGS §4 R04*). Against a salary, those rates are the argument. But T7 will not build a cost comparison, because:

1. The rates are ruled as a **shape**, not confirmed as current figures.
2. An in-house editor's salary varies by country, and this page is read in the US, the UK and Bangladesh. One number would be wrong in two of the three.
3. `instructions.md` §5 rule 3: never invent a figure.

**This is a real gap and it is worth Masud's attention**, because it is the difference between a page that asserts value and one that demonstrates it. → carried as an open item, not a bracket, because nothing on the page is half-written.

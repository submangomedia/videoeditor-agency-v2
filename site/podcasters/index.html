Where podcast production breaks down

  • An hour of footage, every week

    Two or three cameras, an hour each, every single week. The recording is the easy part.

  • Clips are where the audience comes from

    The episode is the product; the clips are the marketing. Cutting five to ten of them per episode is the job nobody has time for.

  • The brand drifts

    Intros, lower-thirds and end cards change a little each week when each episode is built from scratch.

  • Audio is the thing listeners forgive least

    Uneven levels, room noise, a guest on a laptop mic — all fixable in post, and all noticed if they are not.

Record the conversation. We do the rest.

Upload the camera files and the audio. Your dedicated editor cuts the episode with speaker switching, cleans the audio, applies your intro, outro and lower-thirds, and pulls the clips that stand on their own — captioned and sized for each platform.

Revisions are genuinely unlimited. Swap a clip, recut a segment, change the cold open — it is done until you sign it off.

What we edit for podcasters

  • Full-episode multicam edits — speaker switching, branded intro and outro, chapters and end screens.
  • Vertical clips from every episode — captioned shorts for Reels, TikTok, Shorts and LinkedIn, in your template.
  • Intro and lower-third package — built once, applied to every episode, so the show looks like one show.
  • Audio clean-up — levelling, noise reduction, and a guest track rescued where it can be.
  • Episode thumbnails and covers — matched to the show.
  • Audiograms and trailer cuts — for launches and guest promotion.

Questions podcasters ask

  • How do I send an hour of multicam footage?

    A shared Drive, Dropbox or WeTransfer folder. We set one up for you at onboarding, and every episode goes in the same place.

  • How many clips per episode?

    As many as the episode holds. Tell us the number you want to post and we cut to it; if the conversation has more, we say so.

  • Can you match the style of a show I like?

    Send references. We build your own template in that direction — your fonts, your colours, your show's name — rather than copying another show's.

  • Do you handle remote-guest recordings?

    Yes — Riverside, Zoom, StreamYard, separate tracks or a single recording. Separate tracks give the best result.

  • Same editor every episode?

    Yes. One editor learns the show and stays on it.

Why legal video is harder than it looks

  • Careful sentences, impatient platforms

    Lawyers qualify what they say. Short-form punishes that. The edit has to keep the caveat and lose the dead air.

  • A caption is a published statement

    A mis-transcribed word in a caption is a claim you did not make. Legal captions need a human reading against the transcript.

  • Conservative, but not invisible

    The look has to stop a scroll without looking like an ad for a personal-injury hotline.

  • Consistency across dozens of episodes

    Intros, lower-thirds, disclaimers and end cards drift when every episode is edited from scratch.

Record the conversation. We make it publishable.

You send the episode, the talking-head or the recorded webinar. Your dedicated editor cuts the full piece, pulls the clips that stand alone, captions them against your transcript, and applies the firm's template every time — so episode forty looks like episode one.

Revisions are genuinely unlimited. If a clip needs a disclaimer, a caption needs correcting, or a segment should not go out, it is changed.

We edit legal content; we do not advise on attorney advertising rules, which vary by bar and jurisdiction. Where a clip or caption might need review, we flag it and follow your instruction.

Questions law firms ask

  • How do you handle captions?

    From your transcript where you have one; otherwise we transcribe, then a person reads every caption against the audio before delivery. Legal terms are checked, not guessed.

  • Can you add a standard disclaimer to every clip?

    Yes. Give us the wording once and it is part of the template — on screen, in the end card, or both.

  • Is client or case material kept confidential?

    Footage is shared through a private folder, seen only by your assigned editor, and deleted from our systems on request after delivery. Put any specific requirement in the brief.

  • Can you match a template we already use?

    Yes. Send an existing episode or your brand files and we rebuild the template so new episodes match the old ones.

  • Do you work with firms outside Bangladesh?

    Yes — editing is fully remote. Only on-site recording is limited to Dhaka.
